The supplied print takes priority for spelling and verse numbering. Vishvasa and Sanskrit Documents often transmit the same wording; do not count both as two independent manuscript witnesses. The Kannada-script PDF says it was generated from the proofread Devanagari text. It is a transliteration reference, not a Kannada translation.

Follow-up: Kannada meaning

Notes distinguish unchanged Sanskrit from the two corrections verified on the printed pages.

3.26.82: Print p. 55 and 3.26.82 agree. The same epithet occurs at 6.41.46 after Siddhi and Buddhi choose and garland Gaṇeśa as their bridegroom (6.41.33–35). Vishvasa chapter. Parallel in the text.

4.23.13: Print p. 66 and the online text agree. Read the second instrumental phrase with the earlier ‘by him’, and take the following words as a new sentence. Verses 8 and 12 describe why he did not perform the observance. Vishvasa chapter.

6.21.84: Print p. 61 and the online text agree. Verses 85–86 make the comparative sense ‘preferable’ clear, as opposed to ‘a boon’. Vishvasa chapter.

9.6.137: Print p. 23 and the online text agree. 8.46.6–7 and 3.42.34–35 use the same compound with an explicit dual referring to the two poles. Vishvasa chapter. Parallel in the text.

9.4.15: The print and online text agree. At 9.6.95, the same two adjectives are explicitly paired with delusion, and 9.2.25 uses the same contrast. This supports reading the phrase as both with and without delusion. Vishvasa chapter. Parallel in the text.

8.42.27: The first bare letter in print p. 100 has an explicit ablative parallel at 2.16.28. That parallel confirms the first clause, but does not resolve the second bare letter or the force of ‘entered’. Vishvasa chapter. Parallel in the text.

5.24.24: Verses 22–24 identify the previous garland and the alternatives. The scan and online text agree on the final manner adverb, but the exact nuance is still uncertain. Vishvasa chapter.

6.16.71: The print scan and both online texts read garbhāśritam. Verses 62, 65, 74 and 75 and the chapter title establish the inner shrine as the setting. Vishvasa chapter. Print scan.

6.25.9: Print p. 73 and the online text support devanadyai, “to the divine river.” The relation of yad and the identity of the specific river remain uncertain. Vishvasa chapter. Print scan.
